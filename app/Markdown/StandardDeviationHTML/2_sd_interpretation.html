Interpretation

  • Higher standard deviation (of returns) → more volatility (more typical daily swing).
  • Lower standard deviation → calmer price movement (more stable day-to-day).

The idea behind “±1σ”

If returns were perfectly normal (that is, a bell-curve), then:

  • ~68% of returns fall within mean ± 1σ
  • ~95% within mean ± 2σ

But: real stock returns often have fat tails (more extreme moves than normal), so treat this as intuition.

What this MVP plot shows

  • A histogram of daily log returns
  • A line at the mean return
  • Lines at mean − 1σ and mean + 1σ
  • An annualized volatility estimate (σ × √252)

Common Errors

  • Using prices instead of returns (mixes trend with volatility)
  • Comparing different time scales (daily σ vs monthly σ)
  • Assuming normality (markets fluctuate)

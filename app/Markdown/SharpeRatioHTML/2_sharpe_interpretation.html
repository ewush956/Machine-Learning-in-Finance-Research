Interpretation

  • Higher Sharpe ratio means better return per unit of risk.
  • Near zero means performance is close to risk-free.
  • Negative means underperformance versus risk-free after accounting for volatility.

Rule-of-thumb ranges

  • < 0: weak (risk not rewarded)
  • 0 to 1: modest risk-adjusted return
  • 1 to 2: strong
  • > 2: very strong (often hard to sustain)

Important limitations

  • Assumes volatility is a good proxy for risk.
  • Penalizes upside and downside volatility equally.
  • Can be distorted by short samples or outliers.
  • Works best when comparing strategies over similar periods and frequencies.
